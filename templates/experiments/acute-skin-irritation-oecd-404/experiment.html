📐 Calculation & Interpretation

Primary Dermal Irritation Index (PDII)

PDII = (Σ Erythema scores + Σ Oedema scores) / (Number of observations × Number of animals)

* Usually averaged over 24, 48, and 72 h readings.

Interpretation / Classification (GHS)

  • Corrosive (Category 1): Irreversible damage (necrosis, ulcers) within 14 days. Sub-categorized as 1A/1B/1C based on exposure time.
  • Irritant (Category 2): Mean score ≥2.3 – ≤4.0 (erythema or oedema) in ≥2/3 animals, fully reversible within 14 days.
  • Mild Irritant (Category 3): Mean score ≥1.5 – <2.3 (optional).
  • Non-Irritant: Mean score <1.5 or no response.
✖

📋 Procedure (Step-by-Step as per OECD 404)

1. Animal Preparation (24 h before test):

  • Clip the fur from the dorsal/flank region (avoid abrading skin).
  • Select only animals with healthy, intact skin (no dense hair patches).
  • Acclimatize animals to laboratory conditions.

2. Application of Test Substance:

  • Apply 0.5 g or 0.5 mL to a small area (~6 cm²).
  • Cover with gauze patch + semi-occlusive dressing (prevent ingestion/inhalation).
  • Secure with non-irritating adhesive tape.

3. Exposure and Initial Test (1 animal):

  • Stepwise (if corrosion suspected):
    • Patch 1: Remove after 3 minutes.
    • If no corrosion → Patch 2: Remove after 1 hour.
    • If no corrosion → Patch 3: Remove after 4 hours.
  • If corrosion is seen at any stage, terminate the test immediately.
  • If no corrosion expected: Single 4-hour patch on one animal.

4. Patch Removal and Cleaning:

  • After exposure (normally 4 h), remove the patch and gently wash residual substance with water or a suitable solvent (do not alter skin response).

5. Confirmatory Test:

  • If no corrosion in initial animal → Test up to 2 additional animals (each with one 4-hour patch).
  • If responses are equivocal → Use a third animal to confirm results.
