Aim: To study and evaluate the anxiolytic activity of drugs using the Elevated Plus Maze (EPM) method in mice or rats.

Theory/Principle

Principle of EPM

The Elevated Plus Maze (EPM) is a validated behavioral model based on the natural conflict between the tendency of rodents to explore new environments and their innate fear of open and elevated spaces.

Anxiolytic drugs reduce this fear and increase the time spent exploring the open arms of the maze.

Behavioral Parameters

  • Open Arm Exploration: Increased entries and time spent in open arms indicate anxiolytic (anti-anxiety) activity.
  • Closed Arm Preference: Anxious rodents prefer closed arms, which provide shelter and safety.
  • Total Activity: Measured by total arm entries, reflecting general locomotor activity.

Significance

The EPM method is a simple and reliable screening method for evaluating new anxiolytic agents. It is sensitive to GABAA receptor modulators like benzodiazepines (e.g., Diazepam).

Drugs that increase open arm behavior are considered potential candidates for the treatment of generalized anxiety disorders in humans.
