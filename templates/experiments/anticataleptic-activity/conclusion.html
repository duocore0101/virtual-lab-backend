Aim: To study and evaluate anti-catatonic (anti-cataleptic) activity of drugs (e.g., scopolamine) against haloperidol-induced catalepsy in rats/mice.

Conclusion

Result

Administration of haloperidol produced marked catalepsy in rats/mice, as evidenced by prolonged maintenance of imposed posture. Following administration of the test drug, a significant reduction in cataleptic duration was observed when compared to the control group.

Interpretation

Haloperidol-induced catalepsy is primarily due to blockade of dopamine D2 receptors in the nigrostriatal pathway. The reduction in cataleptic duration after treatment indicates a restoration of dopaminergic–cholinergic balance by the test agent.

Summary

The test drug exhibited significant anti-catatonic activity against haloperidol-induced catalepsy. This suggests potential anti-parkinsonian properties and indicates a favorable pharmacological profile regarding motor side effects.
