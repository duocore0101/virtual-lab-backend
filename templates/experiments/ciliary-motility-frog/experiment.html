Aim: To study the effects of drugs on ciliary motility in the frog oesophagus.
✖

Procedure

1. Double-pith or decapitate the frog to destroy the brain and spinal cord (humane euthanasia).

2. Pin the frog supine (on its back) on a frog board.

3. Open the mouth, pin down the lower jaw, and expose the buccal cavity and oesophagus by cutting along the midline if needed. Keep the mucosa moist with frog Ringer's solution.

4. Mark a fixed distance on the oesophagus (e.g., from upper to lower end visible).

5. Control: Apply a few drops of Ringer's/normal saline. Place 5–10 poppy seeds at the upper end. Start stopwatch when seeds begin moving; note time for them to reach the stomach/lower end. Repeat 3 times and calculate mean transit time.

6. Wash thoroughly with saline.

7. Apply drug solutions (a few drops) and wait 5–10 minutes for effect:

  • First: Acetylcholine → repeat transit time measurement.
  • Wash, then physostigmine → wait, measure.
  • Wash, then atropine → wait, measure.

8. For confirmation, repeat after 30 minutes if needed.

(Note: Order may vary; sometimes physostigmine first to show potentiation, atropine last as effect persists.)
