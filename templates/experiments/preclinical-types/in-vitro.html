In Vitro Experiments

Definition

In Vitro experiments are experiments performed outside any living organism in an artificial environment such as glassware or plasticware (test tubes, culture plates, etc.).

Purpose: Quick screening of drug activity on specific biological targets such as receptors, enzymes, or cultured cells.

Step-by-Step Procedure

  • Prepare the test system: Culture cells (e.g., cancer cell lines) or isolated enzymes/proteins in a multi-well plate or test tube.
  • Add known concentrations of the test drug.
  • Incubate under controlled conditions (37°C, specific pH, CO₂).
  • Measure the response using instruments such as spectrophotometer, ELISA reader, or fluorescence reader.
  • Calculate IC₅₀ or EC₅₀ values (concentration producing 50% inhibition or effect).
  • Interpret the data and compare results with standard drugs.

Advantages & Limitations

Advantages

  • Fast and inexpensive
  • High-throughput screening possible
  • Ethical – no animal use
  • Highly controlled experimental conditions

Disadvantages

  • No whole-body effects
  • Does not account for absorption, metabolism, and distribution
  • Results may not always translate directly to humans
