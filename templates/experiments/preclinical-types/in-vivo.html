In Vivo Experiments

Definition

In Vivo experiments are experiments performed on whole living animals under carefully controlled laboratory conditions.

Purpose: To evaluate the overall effect of a drug including absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion (ADME), and the complete physiological response of the body.

Step-by-Step Procedure

  • Select healthy laboratory animals such as rats, mice, or rabbits according to CPCSEA guidelines.
  • Acclimatize animals in the animal house under controlled conditions (temperature, humidity, and 12-hour light/dark cycle).
  • Administer the drug using an appropriate route (oral, intraperitoneal, intravenous, or subcutaneous).
  • Induce the required disease model if necessary (e.g., carrageenan-induced paw edema for anti-inflammatory studies).
  • Record observations at specific time intervals such as behavioral changes, paw volume (plethysmometer), hot-plate latency, rotarod performance, or blood parameters.
  • Collect blood or tissue samples for biochemical or histopathological analysis.
  • Humanely euthanize animals at the end of the experiment and calculate ED₅₀, LD₅₀, and therapeutic index.
  • Perform statistical analysis of the collected data.

Advantages & Limitations

Advantages

  • Most predictive of human physiological response
  • Includes pharmacokinetic processes (ADME)
  • Allows observation of whole-body drug interactions

Disadvantages

  • Expensive and time-consuming
  • Ethical concerns related to animal use
  • Higher biological variability
  • Requires approval from regulatory bodies such as CPCSEA
