🧮 4-Point Bioassay Calculations

1. Randomized Latin Square Design

To minimize bias from time, tachyphylaxis, or sensitivity changes:

Set/Order 1st Dose 2nd Dose 3rd Dose 4th Dose
1S₁S₂T₁T₂
2S₂T₁T₂S₁
3T₁T₂S₁S₂
4T₂S₁S₂T₁
  • Add each dose and record contraction height (cm or units).
  • Wash thoroughly after each response (allow 1–2 min contact time).
  • Repeat sets for 4 cycles and calculate the Mean S₁, Mean S₂, Mean T₁, Mean T₂.

2. Calculation of Potency

The standard formula for 4-point bioassay potency (concentration of unknown):

Potency (Conc. of test) = (n₁ / t₁) × antilog [ ((T₂ - S₂) + (T₁ - S₁)) / ((T₂ - T₁) + (S₂ - S₁)) × log (n₂ / n₁)] × Cₛ

Where:

  • • n₁ / n₂ = volume/dose of lower / higher standard
  • • t₁ / t₂ = volume/dose of lower / higher test
  • • S₁, S₂, T₁, T₂ = mean responses (heights)
  • • Cₛ = concentration of standard ACh solution (μg/ml)

Simplified student formula:

Conc. of unknown = (n₁ / t₁) × antilog {[(T₂ - S₂) + (T₁ - S₁)] / [(T₂ - T₁) + (S₂ - S₁)] × log (n₂ / n₁)} × Cₛ

*This assumes parallelism (dose ratios similar) and log-dose linearity.

📋 Procedure Step-by-Step

1. Preparation of Isolated Rat Ileum:

  • Sacrifice a healthy albino rat (ether/CO₂ anesthesia).
  • Dissect out ileum (avoid Peyer's patches), wash lumen gently with Tyrode's solution.
  • Cut ~2–3 cm segment, tie one end to aerator hook and other to isotonic lever/transducer.
  • Suspend in organ bath containing oxygenated Tyrode's at 37°C.
  • Allow 30–45 min stabilization (wash every 10 min). Tissue shows spontaneous pendular movements.

2. Determine Dose-Response Curve (DRC):

  • Add graded increasing volumes/concentrations of standard ACh (e.g., 0.05–0.5 ml of 1 μg/ml stock).
  • Record contractions until maximal response (ceiling effect).
  • Wash tissue after each response (2–3 times) until baseline returns.

3. Select Doses for 4-Point Assay:

  • From DRC, choose two standard doses (S₁ low, S₂ high) producing responses between ~20–70% of maximum (linear steep part).
  • Choose two test/unknown doses (T₁ low, T₂ high) giving responses bracketing S₁ and S₂ (T₁ response > S₁, T₂ response < S₂; ratio S₂/S₁ ≈ T₂/T₁ for parallelism).
  • Typical example: S₁ = 0.1 ml, S₂ = 0.4 ml of standard; T₁ = 0.15 ml, T₂ = 0.6 ml of test.

4. Record Responses in Latin Square Design:

  • Use a 4×4 Latin square for randomization (to minimize bias from time/tachyphylaxis/sensitivity changes).
