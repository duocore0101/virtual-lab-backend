{% load static %} Conduction Anaesthetic Activity – Theory
Aim: To study conduction anaesthetic activity by loss of reflex withdrawal in frog.

Theory/Principle

Principle

Conduction anaesthesia is produced by injecting a local anaesthetic near a nerve trunk or plexus, thereby blocking impulse conduction along the nerve.

Local anaesthetics reversibly inhibit voltage-gated Na⁺ channels in the sciatic nerve, preventing transmission of sensory impulses from the foot to the spinal cord.

This results in loss or delay of withdrawal reflex when the foot is exposed to a painful stimulus.

Animal: Frog
Nerve Studied: Sciatic nerve
Stimulus: Chemical (Dilute HCl)
Observation: Withdrawal reflex

Procedure

1. Take pithed/decerebrated frog (destroy brain/upper spinal cord ethically).

2. Expose sciatic nerve plexus (near thigh root) via small skin incision.

3. Check baseline: Dip one foot in dilute acid (0.1–0.2% HCl) → brisk withdrawal reflex (time <5 sec).

4. Inject 0.2–0.5 mL test drug (e.g., 1% procaine) around sciatic plexus (perineural).

5. Inject saline on other leg (control).

6. Test every 2–5 min: Dip foot in acid; record time to withdrawal.

7. Measure onset (no/reflex delayed >10 sec) and duration (until reflex returns).

8. Alternative: Isolate sciatic-gastrocnemius preparation; stimulate nerve electrically and record muscle twitch abolition.
