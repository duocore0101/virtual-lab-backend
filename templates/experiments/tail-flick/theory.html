Aim: To Study and Evaluate Analgesic Activity by Tail Flick Method

Theory/Principle

Principle

The tail flick method (D’Amour & Smith, 1941) is used to screen centrally acting analgesics. A radiant heat stimulus (focused light beam from analgesiometer) is applied to the distal part of the tail. The animal responds by flicking/withdrawing the tail (spinal reflex modulated by supraspinal centres). Analgesics increase the latency period by raising the pain threshold via central mechanisms. The vehicle itself produces no change in latency, validating it as control. Cut-off time prevents tissue damage.
