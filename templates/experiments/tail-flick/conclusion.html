Aim: To Study and Evaluate Analgesic Activity by Tail Flick Method

Result & Conclusion

Result

The mean reaction time (latency) in the vehicle-treated control group remained almost unchanged (≈ 4.3 sec) throughout the 120 min period. The Morphine-treated group showed a significant increase in latency, peaking at 11.8 sec (≈ 195% increase) at 60 min. This confirms potent central analgesic activity of morphine compared to the vehicle control.

Conclusion

Morphine sulphate (standard opioid analgesic) produced a marked and statistically significant increase in tail flick latency (pain threshold) compared to the vehicle-treated control group, demonstrating central analgesic activity. It acts primarily by stimulating μ-opioid receptors in the CNS, modulating spinal reflexes and raising pain threshold. The vehicle (normal saline) itself had no effect on latency, validating the control.
