Aim: To study the effects of drugs on Rabbit Eye.
✖

Instructions

  1. Place rabbit in restrainer
  2. Record baseline pupil diameter
  3. Test corneal and light reflex
  4. Instill pilocarpine and observe miosis
✖

Procedure

1. Select and restrain the animal: Gently place the rabbit in a restrainer or holding box to immobilize it without causing stress. Ensure the head is steady and eyes are accessible.

2. Observe and record baseline (normal) parameters: In normal room light, observe both eyes.

  • Note pupil size (normal ~4-6 mm).
  • Test direct light reflex: Shine torch light into one eye → observe constriction in the same eye.
  • Test consensual light reflex: Shine light into one eye → observe constriction in the opposite eye.
  • Measure pupil diameter horizontally using a ruler held close to the eye.

3. Instill the test drug:

  • Use one eye as treated and the other as control (instill normal saline in control).
  • Gently pull down the lower eyelid to expose the conjunctival sac.
  • Instill 1-2 drops of the drug solution into the lower conjunctival sac.
  • Release the eyelid and allow the rabbit to blink for even distribution. Avoid touching the cornea.

4. Observe and record changes at intervals: Observe both eyes at 0, 5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60 minutes (or until peak effect).

  • Measure pupil diameter.
  • Test direct and consensual light reflexes.
  • Note onset, peak, and duration of effect.
  • Compare treated vs. control eye.

5. Repeat with different drugs: Allow recovery (several hours or next day) between drugs. Test multiple mydriatics and miotics sequentially.

6. Wash the eye if needed: At the end, flush with normal saline to reverse effects (especially miotics).
