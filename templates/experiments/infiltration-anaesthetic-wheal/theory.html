{% load static %} Infiltration Anaesthetic Activity – Theory
Aim: To study infiltration anaesthetic activity by loss of cutaneous sensation in guinea pig wheal.

Theory / Principle

Principle

Infiltration anaesthesia is produced when a local anaesthetic is injected intradermally or subcutaneously, blocking sensory nerve endings at the site of injection.

Local anaesthetics act by reversible inhibition of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels in peripheral nerve fibers, preventing conduction of pain impulses from the skin.

Animal: Guinea Pig
Site: Dorsal skin (wheal formation)
Stimulus: Pin prick
Observation: Loss of cutaneous sensation

Loss of response to pinprick in the wheal area indicates effective infiltration anaesthesia.
