Theory/Principle

Principle

Oxytocin produces dose-dependent rhythmic contractions of uterine smooth muscle by acting on oxytocin receptors (Gq-coupled).

Activation of Gq receptors leads to increased intracellular Ca²⁺ concentration, resulting in smooth muscle contraction.

The isolated rat uterine horn (especially from estrogen-primed or oestrus-phase female rats) is highly sensitive to oxytocin.

In the interpolation method, a full dose-response curve (DRC) is recorded using standard oxytocin. The response (contraction height) is plotted against log dose.

The response of the unknown test sample is then interpolated on the linear portion (20–80%) of the standard curve to estimate its concentration.

Important: Responses must fall within the linear range of the DRC for accurate potency determination.
