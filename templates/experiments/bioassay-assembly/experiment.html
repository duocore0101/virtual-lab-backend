Magnification Values in Student Organ Bath Assembly

Magnification value in a student organ bath assembly refers to the amplification factor provided by the recording lever (usually a frontal writing lever or simple/isotonic lever). This magnifies the small contractions of the tissue (e.g., guinea pig ileum) so they are clearly visible on the smoked/inked drum paper.

Formula for Magnification (M)

M = A / B (or M = d(F–W) / d(F–T))
  • A or d(F–W): Distance from fulcrum (F) to writing stylus/point (W) (longer arm, in cm)
  • B or d(F–T): Distance from fulcrum (F) to tissue attachment point (T) (shorter arm, in cm)

This is based on the principle of levers (first-class lever system).
Example: If the longer arm (fulcrum to stylus) = 10 cm and the shorter arm (fulcrum to tissue thread attachment) = 2 cm, then M = 10 / 2 = 5 times (5× magnification).

How to Calculate During Assembly Setup (Step-by-Step)

  • 1. Assemble the organ bath: Fill with physiological salt solution (e.g., Tyrode’s), maintain temperature (usually 37°C), aerate (95% O₂ + 5% CO₂ or air), and mount the isolated tissue (one end fixed at the bottom, other end tied with thread to the lever).
  • 2. Fix the lever: Fix the lever on a stand/clamp so the writing stylus lightly touches the kymograph drum.
  • 3. Adjust fulcrum: Adjust the fulcrum position along the lever (or choose lever arm lengths) to get the required magnification for the specific tissue.
  • 4. Measure distances: Use a ruler or scale to measure Distance A (fulcrum → stylus tip) and Distance B (fulcrum → point where tissue thread is tied).
  • 5. Calculate: Calculate M = A / B.
  • 6. Balance the lever: Add plasticine/weight on the longer arm so the stylus rests lightly on the drum with proper tension on the tissue (usually 0.5–1 g load).
  • 7. Test: Test by gently pulling the tissue thread – the stylus movement on the drum should be M times larger than actual tissue shortening.
  • 8. Record & Adjust: Record a baseline and adjust fulcrum height/position if needed until desired M is achieved.

Typical Recommended Magnification Values

  • Guinea pig ileum: 5–10×
  • Rat uterus: 4–6×
  • Frog rectus abdominis: 10×
  • Rat fundus strip: 10–15×

* Lower magnification for highly contractile/rhythmic tissues; higher for less contractile ones to get clear, measurable tracings without overshooting the drum.
