🧮 Step-by-Step Calculation (Standard 3-Point Formula)

The most common formula in B.Pharm practicals (as per Kulkarni, RJPT, and lab manuals):

Concentration of unknown (test) = (s₁ / t) × antilog[ (T – S₁) / (S₂ – S₁) × log(s₂ / s₁) ] × Conc. of standard stock

Where:

  • s₁ = volume of low standard (S1) = 0.2 mL
  • s₂ = volume of high standard (S2) = 0.6 mL
  • t = volume of test (T) = 0.4 mL
  • S₁ = average response of S1 = 23 mm
  • S₂ = average response of S2 = 57 mm
  • T = average response of test = 42 mm (use 41.67 for precision)
  • Conc. of standard stock = 10 μg/mL
1. Calculate ratio fraction: (T – S₁) / (S₂ – S₁) = (42 – 23) / (57 – 23) = 19 / 34 ≈ 0.5588
2. log(s₂ / s₁): log(0.6 / 0.2) = log(3) ≈ 0.4771
3. Multiply: 0.5588 × 0.4771 ≈ 0.2666
4. Antilog: Antilog(0.2666) ≈ 1.847 (use calculator or log table)
5. Volume ratio: (s₁ / t) = 0.2 / 0.4 = 0.5
6. Potency ratio: 0.5 × 1.847 ≈ 0.9235
7. Concentration of unknown: 0.9235 × 10 μg/mL ≈ 9.24 μg/mL

(If using exact 41.67 mm for T: (41.67 – 23)/(57 – 23) = 18.67/34 ≈ 0.5491; then 0.5491 × 0.4771 ≈ 0.262; antilog ≈ 1.828; 0.5 × 1.828 ≈ 0.914; 0.914 × 10 ≈ 9.14 μg/mL — minor difference, report as ≈9.2 μg/mL)

📋 Procedure (Simplified Step-by-Step)

1. Animal Sacrifice and Tissue Isolation:

  • Sacrifice the rat by cervical dislocation or stunning blow + exsanguination (as per ethical guidelines).
  • Open the abdomen with midline incision.
  • Remove the entire stomach carefully.
  • Identify the fundus (upper greyish portion). Cut along the junction to separate fundus from antrum/body.
  • Rinse the fundus gently with Krebs' solution to remove contents.
  • Open the fundus along the greater curvature.
  • Prepare a zig-zag (zigzag) strip of longitudinal muscle (1–1.5 cm wide, 3–4 cm long) by making alternate cuts to increase surface area and sensitivity.
  • Tie one end with thread for attachment to aerator hook (lower end) and the other to recording lever/transducer (upper end).

2. Mounting the Tissue in Organ Bath:

  • Suspend the strip in aerated Krebs' solution (5–10 mL bath) at 37°C.
  • Apply initial load/tension of ~1 g (using spring-loaded lever or weight).
  • Allow 30–60 min equilibration with frequent washing (every 10–15 min) until baseline stabilizes and spontaneous contractions subside.
  • The tissue is very sensitive; avoid overstretching.

3. Dose-Response Curve (Preliminary):

  • Add graded doses of standard 5-HT (e.g., 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 ng/mL bath concentration).
  • Contact time: 90–120 seconds (contraction peaks slowly; 90% response in 90–120 s).
  • Wash out after each response (2–3 washes) and allow 3–5 min recovery.
  • Record height of contraction (mm on kymograph or digital units).
  • Plot preliminary log dose-response curve and identify:
    • S1: Low dose giving small but measurable response (~20–40%).
    • S2: Higher dose giving larger response (~60–80%).
    • T: Test dose giving response between S1 and S2.

4. Actual Three-Point Bioassay:

  • Use selected doses: S1, S2, T.
  • Add doses in randomized/bracketing sequence to minimize bias (e.g., over 3 cycles):
    • Cycle 1: S1 → S2 → T
    • Cycle 2: S2 → T → S1
    • Cycle 3: T → S1 → S2
  • Maintain constant contact time (90–120 s), wash thoroughly, and fixed time interval between doses (usually 5–8 min).
  • Record all responses.
